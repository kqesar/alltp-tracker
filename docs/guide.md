## A Link to the Past

**Items.** Click a cell to cycle its state, right-click to step it back. Progressive items step through their levels (sword 0→4, gloves 0→2) and wrap round; the rest toggle on and off. Greyed means not obtained, or for a boss not yet beaten. **Undo / Redo** (Ctrl/Cmd+Z, Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z) take back any change, a new game included.

**The board.** Five rows of items come first: the Y items and the equipment, then a row with the bombs, the four bottles and what Ganon's Tower and Ganon ask for in crystals. Under them are the dungeons, by world. The first row holds the Light World's three (EP, DP, ToH), Castle Tower (Agahnim, CT) and Ganon's Tower (GT), then the pendants and the crystals you hold. The second row holds the seven crystals' dungeons, PoD to TR. Each dungeon's short name sits in its cell's top left corner, which matters with shuffled bosses, where a boss's portrait no longer tells its dungeon. While the key cells show, each dungeon's sits under it, with Hyrule Castle's (HC) at the end of the Light World's row.

**Bottles.** The four bottle cells show what each bottle holds: empty, a red, green or blue potion, a fairy, a bee, a good bee or a mushroom (which the game's menu also draws in a bottle). A bottle you have not found is greyed. A click on one finds the bottles up to it, as the game fills them in order; on a bottle you have, a click steps what it holds and goes round back to empty after the mushroom. A right-click steps it back, and from empty the bottle goes. By voice, say "tracker second bottle" (or "bottle two"): it finds that bottle, then steps what it holds, never round to empty. Autotracking reads the bottles and what they hold from the game, in Race Mode too (v2.0 lets a tracker show "bottle contents"). Each is the game's own sprite, as its item menu draws that bottle; the bee and the good bee differ there by the good bee's "+".

**Bomb and arrow capacity.** The bombs' cell counts how many bombs you can hold: 10 at the start, 50 at most (shown in green). A click adds an upgrade of 5, round from 50 back to 10, and a right-click takes one off. The arrows' count works the same way, from 30 to 70, in the bow's bottom right corner. By voice, say "tracker bomb upgrade" (or "bomb bag") and "tracker arrow upgrade" (or "quiver"). Autotracking reads both outside Race Mode only: v2.0 names the bomb and arrow counts but not the capacity upgrades, and what it does not name it forbids. In a race, they are yours to mark.

**Crystals.** The GT and Ganon cells show how many crystals Ganon's Tower opens with and how many Ganon asks for. The number turns green once the crystals you hold reach it, and the crystal cell under them shows how many you hold ("4/7"). The numbers are the run's settings, the same as in **Mode**. A seed sets them outside Race Mode; in a race (and in a Mystery run, "?" until known) you set them by reading the signs in the game. A click adds one, round from 7 to 0, a right-click takes one off, and from "?" a click starts at 0. The pendant cell beside the crystals lights the green, blue and red pendants you hold. Both count the prizes you marked on the bosses you beat, or what autotracking read.

**Map.** Every marker is colour-coded against the items you hold:

| Colour | Glyph | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| green | ✓ | reachable now |
| yellow | ? | maybe reachable (an unknown medallion, a key that may be anywhere), or only some of a marker's checks |
| orange | heart | doable now, outside Basic's safety margin: out of logic only because you lack the hearts, bottles, stronger weapon or protection the **Basic** item placement waits for (Basic runs only, see **Basic placement's safety margin**) |
| purple | lightning bolt | out of logic, doable with a trick you know (only while the tricks are on, see **Tricks**) |
| light blue | eye | seen but not reachable yet (the Book reads the Pedestal, a ledge item shows from below) |
| red | ✕ | out of logic |
| grey | | already done |

The glyphs mean colour is never the only cue. Click a marker to check its locations (a cave's chests together), or a boss to mark it beaten. Hover or focus a marker to list its checks and their state in a tooltip, and a dungeon's items left; a check not in logic says what it is missing ("Missing: Hammer, or Moon Pearl + Power Glove") and why one is only maybe reachable. A dungeon's tooltip groups its checks by what they are missing, one line per thing, a room that holds several named once with its count: "Lantern: Big Chest, Dark Maze ×2, Boss". It takes what the tracker has already worked out, never solving anything as you hover: until it is ready it says "Working it out…". On a touch screen, press and hold. **Map: Auto / Side / Stack** sets how the two worlds sit: Auto keeps them side by side while there is room and stacks them on a narrow screen; the choice is remembered on this device.

**Tricks.** The logic never counts on a trick, and nothing here changes it. Turn the tricks on and a check that is out of logic but that a trick you know would open turns purple with a lightning bolt, "doable with a trick", on its marker, in the Checks list and in a dungeon's square or boss; it comes after available, maybe and Basic's safety margin (orange, see below) and before light blue and red, so something in logic or maybe in logic never hides behind a trick. The six tricks are the minor ones the randomizer's own glitched logic counts: dark rooms without the Lamp, fake flipper, water walk, super bunny, bunny revival and dungeon revival. **Mode**, under **Show key cells**, has a **Tricks** button opening the dialog: a switch ("Mark checks these tricks open"), one checkbox per trick you know (all six once it is on; untick the ones you cannot do) with a line on what it does and links to a video or page that teaches it, which open in a new tab. A check a trick opens says which: in the Checks list its row names them under the check, ringed in purple ("Fake flipper"), without opening anything; "Doable now with: Fake flipper, or Water walk + Fake flipper" (every set of the tricks you know that opens it on its own, the fewest first) leads its lines in the tooltip (a long press on a phone), by name, and in **Why?** and a dungeon's **What's missing?**, where each name links to the trick's guide; a dungeon's grouped lines lead with it ("Doable now with Dark rooms without the Lamp; or Lantern: Big Chest, Dark Maze ×2"). **Why?** has an **About tricks** button opening the dialog. The legend gains the purple state while the tricks are on, and **Checks** a **Trick** filter with its count (not shown at first, like **Seen**). Nothing is read from the game, so Race Mode is unchanged; the choice is kept on the device and is no part of the run, an export or a shared link. It marks nothing in a run with shuffled entrances or whose logic is not modelled, a glitched seed's included, and says so in the dialog. alttpr.com has no logic of these tricks alone (its Minor Glitches logic went in VT8.25): Overworld Glitches counts four of them, but with boots clips, mirror clips and super speed besides, so a NoGlitches seed is where the tricks show, always out of logic. "Doable" is the glitched logic's meaning: a trick branch keeps its own conditions (a bottle and the net for bunny revival, taking no damage for some fake flippers), and the dark rooms trick stands in for the Lamp in every dark room, the Fire Rod's rooms included.

**Basic placement's safety margin.** The **Basic** item placement asks more of Link than the **Advanced** one before the randomizer puts something you need behind a place, so that a newer player is never asked to scrape through. Those extra demands are a promise about where items are, not a wall: the Advanced placement counts the same places in logic without them, so you can do them now. The tracker treats these as Basic's safety margins:

- a dark world dungeon's way in: 7 hearts on screen (the 3 you start with count), a sword and a bottle before Palace of Darkness, Swamp Palace, Skull Woods and Thieves' Town; 12 hearts, the Master Sword, and two bottles or the Blue Mail before Ice Palace, Misery Mire, Turtle Rock and Ganon's Tower;
- a boss's weapon: the Master Sword (or, for some, the bow, the Fire Rod with magic, or the Cape or Cane of Byrna with a sword for Blind) for Helmasaur King, Arrghus, Mothula, Blind, Kholdstare and Vitreous, the Tempered Sword (or the Master Sword with magic) for Trinexx;
- Turtle Rock's laser bridge: the Cape, the Cane of Byrna or the Mirror Shield against the lasers;
- Ganon: the Tempered Sword where the Master Sword would do, and the silver arrows (a swordless run needs them whatever the placement, so they stay required there).

A check, a dungeon's square or its boss that is out of logic for those margins alone turns orange with a heart, "doable, outside Basic's safety margin", instead of red. It comes after available and maybe and before a trick, seen and out of logic: it asks for nothing you lack but the margin, and no technique to learn. The tooltip, **Why?** and a dungeon's **What's missing?** lead with "Doable now, outside Basic's safety margin" and say which margin and what the seed waits for, with your hearts when hearts are part of it: "Basic placement waits for 12 hearts (you have 11 hearts) before the seed relies on entering Misery Mire", "… waits for Master Sword before the seed relies on beating Arrghus", "… waits for Cane of Byrna, or Magic Cape, or Mirror Shield before the seed relies on crossing Turtle Rock's laser bridge". The go mode does the same: when every step to the goal is open but for the margins (Ganon's included), it reads ★♥ **Go mode: outside Basic's margin** and its panel lists what is left. What else Basic placement asks is about getting through, not about taking hits or hitting harder, and stays out of logic as before: the Pegasus Boots where the Hookshot crosses a gap, the Fire Rod or Ether at Ganon's Tower's Bob's Chest, the Book at the Pedestal, the Magic Mirror to bring the smith back, the Hookshot at the Bumper Cave, and Ice Palace's keys; so does anything else missing, a dark room's Lamp included. The legend shows the orange state in a Basic run, and **Checks** gains an **Outside margin** filter with its count (not shown at first). It changes nothing in an Advanced run, while the item placement of a Mystery run is not known (those places are maybe already), in a run with shuffled entrances, or in one whose logic is not modelled. The logic itself is untouched: the map, the go mode and Race Mode read the same rules, this only says why something is out of logic.

**Checks.** The **Checks** tab beside **Map** lists every check of the map as a list, by zone, in the same states. It shows what is available or maybe at first; the filters (with their counts), a zone and a search narrow it. Tick a check to mark it done, as a click on its marker does; **Why?** says what a check not available is missing, and a dungeon's heading has **What's missing?**, the same grouped lines for the whole dungeon, whichever checks the filters show. A dungeon you track by its counter alone shows its checks done once the counter reaches 0 ("no item left" beside its name), but for its boss's, which stays open until you mark the boss beaten. Once one of its checks is ticked (autotracking ticks each one outside Race Mode), every check shows its own state instead: at 0 a chest not opened yet holds only the dungeon's map, compass or keys, and stays unticked until you open it. The tab you leave open is remembered on this device.

**Checks that cost rupees.** A check the game sells shows its price in parentheses after its name, on the map marker's name and tooltip, in the Checks list and **Why?**, in the run's finds, in the autotracking readout and in a review: "Bottle Merchant (100 rupees)". They are the Bottle Merchant (100), the Chest Game (30), the Digging Game (80), King Zora (500), the Blacksmith (10) and, in an Inverted run, the Bomb Merchant (100, for the big bomb). The price is only a note: the logic does not count rupees, so such a check is as available as a free one whether or not you have the money yet.

**Run.** The **Run** tab beside **Checks** follows the run as it goes:
- **At a glance**: the run timer's time, the checks done and the bosses beaten.
- **Splits**: each boss in the order it fell, with the time since the last, as a speedrun timer splits a run.
- **Timeline**: every item found and location checked, newest first, timed by the run timer. Whatever you or autotracking marks is timed. Undo takes a time away with its mark, and Redo brings the same time back. In Race Mode it lists the items alone: the race rules allow a timeline of items but never let a tracker tie an item to the place it came from, which a place's time beside it would. So it does while autotracking, set to connect, has not read the seed's race flag yet. The places keep their times, and show again once Race Mode is off.
- **From the game**: outside Race Mode, while autotracking reads your game, the counts its ROM keeps, as its end credits list them: when the first sword, the boots, the flute and the mirror were found, deaths, save and quits, menu and lag time, and more.
- **Review**: once the run is over, the seed's spoiler log set against it (see [Reviewing a run](#reviewing-a-run)).

The timeline is saved with the run, in exports and shared links too, which is why **Export** and **Share** are off in Race Mode (see [Races](#autotracking) below and [Sharing a run](#sharing-a-run)).

**Go mode.** Beside the tabs, ☆ **Go mode: not yet**, ★? **possible**, ★♥ **outside Basic's margin** (every step open but for the Basic placement's safety margin, see above) or ★ **in logic** tells whether every step to the run's goal (Ganon, fast Ganon or the Pedestal) is in logic. Not yet, it says how far you are once it has worked it out: **2 items left** (the fewest items any way takes) or **crystals left**. Click it to see the items still missing, crystals aside, since you count those on the board (the go mode still needs them): one item a line when there is one way, else each way on a row, the fewest items first, three in sight and the others behind **N more ways**. The items are named and drawn as on the grid, levels by name (Master Sword, Titan's Mitt), hearts as hearts on screen, keys and medallions where the logic wants them. "Only crystals left" when nothing else is; a sentence when no list can say it: more than three items away, nothing left to get but keys that may sit out of reach, or no item settling it while a setting is not known yet or it hangs on where an item sits. It reads only what you marked and the rules, nothing from the game, so it is the same in Race Mode. A Triforce Hunt's go mode is its count: **7 pieces left** until the pieces its goal asks for are found, then **in logic** (see **Triforce Hunt**). The goal that ends with everything (all dungeons) has none.

**Run timer.** Beside go mode, the run's time: click it to start, pause, resume or reset it. While autotracking reads your game, it starts as you leave the file select for the game and stops at the goal on its own (**Start and stop with the game**, on by default; connecting mid-run, or resetting while the game plays, starts nothing, and the panel says so: start it yourself; the goal ends the run all the same, untimed if the timer never started, which opens the review). It keeps counting through a reload. **End run**, in the timer's panel while it counts or is paused, stops the timer for good once you confirm (Cancel is the focused choice): for a run tracked by hand, or one that ends short of the goal. **Reset** or **New game** starts again.

The timer's **Clock** choice picks what it counts. **Real time** (the default) is the time that passes, as races time it. **Game time** is the game's own play time, counted by the ROM frame by frame (menus and lag included, as the end credits show it): while autotracking reads your game outside Race Mode, with a file loaded, the timer is set on it at each reading and ticks between two, stops while the game is before play (the spawn select) or its counter stands still (a paused emulator), and stops at the goal on the game's final time. The game then drives the timer, so Start, Pause and **Start and stop with the game** are off; at the title or the file select, where the game holds no time, the timer pauses. In Race Mode, or until a game is read, the timer counts real time and the panel says why. Switching the choice mid-run keeps the timer's value: to Game time it jumps to the game's time at the next reading (a timer already stopped at the goal on real time takes the game's final time), back to Real time it goes on from what it shows. Outside Game time the panel also gives the game's own play time. The stream view's timer and the Run tab follow whichever time the timer shows, and so does the overlay API outside Race Mode (in it, the API tells no time).

**Mystery.** The Mystery run type starts with its settings hidden: the goal, the swords, the crystals for Ganon's Tower and for Ganon, the item placement, pool and functionality, the accessibility and the boss shuffle. Set each one in the run type dialog's summary as you learn it (a sign, Uncle's gift, the first key found outside its dungeon…), your progress kept, and pick "? Unknown" to hide one again. Outside Race Mode, autotracking knows a Mystery seed by its file on alttpr.com: a fresh board switches to Mystery, in the world it holds. In Race Mode it asks alttpr.com for nothing, so pick Mystery yourself.

**Boss shuffle.** alttpr.com's enemizer can move the dungeons' bosses: **Simple** (each boss once, Armos, Lanmolas and Moldorm twice), **Full** (each once, three more at random) or **Random** (any, repeats allowed). Set it under **Boss shuffle** in the run type dialog's summary, or start from the seed (its file on alttpr.com says it; the ROM does not, short of naming the bosses, which the tracker never reads before you meet them). While a dungeon's boss is not known, the tracker reads it as any boss that dungeon can hold (Tower of Hera and Ganon's Tower's top never hold Armos, Arrghus, Blind, Lanmolas or Trinexx; Skull Woods never Trinexx; Ganon's Tower's middle never Blind; in a swordless seed Kholdstare stays in Ice Palace): the boss, its prize and the go mode are in logic once you can beat every one of them, maybe once you can beat some, out of logic when you can beat none. Agahnim and Agahnim 2 never move. A Mystery run hides the setting until you set it, and reads every boss that way meanwhile. Once you know a dungeon's boss, say it: its boss cell gains a small **?** in its top left corner while the run's bosses may move; each click steps through the bosses that dungeon can hold (a right-click or "-" back), drawn as the boss, and from then on that dungeon's logic is exactly that boss's. A keyboard shortcut can step it (Shortcuts, under each dungeon: **Known boss**), and so can your voice: "tracker mire blind", "tracker hera cold stare" (the names are said in words the speech model knows: "armor knights", "lan molas", "mold dorm", "helma saur", "argus", "moth hula", "blind", "cold stare", "vitreous", "tri necks"); a boss the dungeon cannot hold is refused. With **Simple**, which places each boss once (Armos, Lanmolas and Moldorm twice), a boss you have named in as many dungeons as it comes is no longer one the others wait for. Outside Race Mode, autotracking names a dungeon's boss the moment you walk into its boss room and the boss is there, and keeps it; a boss you named yourself stays yours (**Sync from game** sets it to the game's). It never reads which bosses the seed placed before you meet them, and in Race Mode it reads none (the race admins' clarification of June 2026 forbids telling which room you are in): name them yourself. Ganon's Tower's three bosses have no cell yet, and stay read as any boss they can be. A run with shuffled entrances and shuffled bosses has no logic yet: every check shows as maybe, and the map says so.
- **Until a setting is known**, the map is cautious. A check that depends on it is *maybe*, never *available*, and anything shown available or out of logic is so whatever it turns out to be. The go mode says "goal unknown" until the goal is set.
- **The world and the dungeon items** are not hidden: you see the world on the first screen, and the dungeon items count each dungeon's items, so the run assumes them until you set them. A change of dungeon items keeps each counter's items found.
- **With autotracking outside Race Mode**, the seed's settings are set on their own as it is read. In Race Mode they are not read, so they stay yours to find.

**Retro.** The bow shoots rupees once you buy an arrow, and a small key opens any dungeon's door. A row under the grid holds an **Arrow shop** and a **Key shop**: mark each when you find a shop that sells it (voice: "tracker arrow shop", "tracker key shop"). Until then the bow shoots only with the silver arrows, and a door behind a small key is *maybe*: the tracker cannot tell which dungeon a key you found was meant for. Once a key shop is found, every key door is in logic, as the randomizer has it.

**Crosskeys: the entrance assistant.** A run with shuffled entrances shows every door and hole the shuffle mixes on the map: an empty dashed ring while you do not know where it leads, filled once you note it, with a glyph for the kind of place (D a dungeon, ↔ a cave with several doors, • a shop or house, ▼ where a hole drops). Click one, search, and pick where it leads: only what the shuffle may put there is offered, a place noted elsewhere says so, and picking it moves it. A hole and the door of the cave it drops into are noted together. The checks inside caves, houses and dungeons leave their usual spots: each place's stand above the first door you note to lead there, so the map shows what you can reach through the doors you know. Pointing at a noted door rings every other door into the same place. The **Entrances** tab lists them all by world, with how many are noted. They are saved with the run and undone like anything else. The assistant knows the Crossworld and Full shuffles (Open, Retro and Standard worlds); for Simple, Restricted and Insanity, or in the Inverted world, the tab says it has no list yet rather than showing another shuffle's, and the map draws no doors.

**Crosskeys: the logic.** The map follows the logic of the entrance randomizer that fills a Crosskeys seed, its own rules and not the Open world's, through the doors you noted: a check behind a door you have not noted stays out of logic, and a note over the map says so. Note a door and what lies behind it turns available as soon as you can reach it, dungeons and caves included, and the go mode steps through the same world. The logic loads with a Crosskeys run alone. Other shuffles, Inverted's crossed one, and a Crosskeys run whose keys stay in their dungeons still show every check as *maybe*. **Why?** and the tooltips say what a check is missing in items, the events and places on the way included (the floodgate, the frog, a region a door rule asks for), or, when no item would bring it, that it waits on a door you have not noted.

**Boss overlays.** Each boss cell carries three corner buttons: the medallion requirement (top right, Misery Mire and Turtle Rock only), the remaining chest count (bottom left) and the reward, crystal or pendant (bottom right). Click steps them forward, right-click back. Autotracking fills the medallion in outside Race Mode once the game shows it (see "The board follows the game").

**Triforce Hunt.** A Triforce Hunt run (the run type of that name) adds a cell under the grid, in the middle (between the shops in a Retro hunt): a Triforce piece with the pieces you found over the pieces the goal asks for, "12/20", the count in white until you hold enough, then in the green of a key count at its top. A click adds a piece, a right-click or "-" takes one off (it stops at 0 and never wraps round), and by voice "tracker tri force" (also "try force" or "tri force piece") adds one; its keyboard shortcut is yours to set in the Shortcuts dialog. alttpr.com's Triforce Hunt hides 30 pieces among the items and asks for 20 (the randomizer's `config/alttp.php`); a customized seed may ask for another number, which autotracking reads from the ROM. The **Go mode** button counts the pieces left ("Go mode: 7 pieces left"), and is **in logic** once you hold the pieces asked for: they are brought to Murahdahla at Hyrule Castle, and the randomizer's rules place the pieces among the items like any other, so the tracker cannot tell where they are, only how many you have. Autotracking sets the pieces from the game, in Race Mode too (v2.0 lists "Triforce pieces" among the inventory items a tracker may auto-track); the pieces asked for come from the ROM's settings, so Race Mode leaves them at 20 and a seed that asks for another number is yours to know. The cell is not shown while a Mystery run's goal is hidden. Old saves and links read no piece.

**Hearts.** The heart in the tunic's cell (bottom right) counts your hearts as the game's pause menu shows them: the hearts on screen, 3 to 20, as a number, and the heart pieces toward the next one as the heart behind it, filled a quarter per piece in the menu's order (top left, bottom left, top right). The first click on **?** sets the 3 hearts you start with; each click after adds a piece, the fourth making a heart as in the game, and a right-click or "-" takes a piece off (it stops at **?** and at 20, never wrapping round). By voice, "tracker heart" (or "heart container") adds a whole heart, keeping the pieces, and "tracker heart piece" (or "piece of heart") adds a piece; from **?** each counts the 3 you start with and the find. Its name says both, "Hearts: 13 and 2 quarters". It shows **?** until you set it, and the tracker then reads your hearts as unknown. They matter with the **Basic** item placement, whose dungeons the randomizer opens only once you have 7 hearts on screen, the 3 you start with included (Palace of Darkness, Swamp Palace, Skull Woods and Thieves' Town, with a sword and a bottle; Ice Palace, Misery Mire, Turtle Rock and Ganon's Tower want 12, with the Master Sword and two bottles or a better mail): with your hearts unknown those dungeons show as maybe, never out of logic, and **Why?** says "it takes 7 hearts (Basic placement), and your hearts are not set"; once set, a dungeon short of them is out of logic, shown orange as doable outside Basic's safety margin (see **Basic placement's safety margin**), and says "7 hearts" among what the seed waits for. Autotracking sets the hearts and the pieces from the game, in Race Mode too (v2.0 lets a tracker react to the heart count and the heart piece count). The logic counts each piece as a quarter of a heart, as the randomizer does; a gate asks for whole hearts, so a piece only matters once four make one. The escape's Ten Bombs, which no cell records, read the same way until autotracking reads them from the game: maybe, never out of logic.

**Half Magic.** The ½ cell sits between the tunic and the boots, as in other trackers: a click, or `/`, toggles it. The game's own reading marks it too. A quarter magic, which only a customized seed gives, shows as the ½, while the logic keeps the real value. Agahnim, named CT, heads Castle Tower's column in the Light World's row, over its keys; the GT cell beside it is Ganon's Tower: Agahnim 2 and the items it has left.

**Keyboard.** The item grid is a single Tab stop: arrow keys move inside it (through a boss's overlays too) and wrap, Home / End jump to the ends of a row, Ctrl+Home / End to the ends of the grid. Space and Enter step the focused control forward, "-" steps it back. Every map marker is a button Tab can reach; "Skip to map" and "Skip to checks" jump to either view.

**Shortcuts.** Keys step the board without the mouse. While the tracker has the focus (and no field or dialog does), a key steps its control forward as a click does, and Shift with the same key steps it back as a right-click does; a held key steps once. The defaults are the keys the tracker has always given: the items run down the left hand's Q, A and Z rows and on across the right hand's Y and H rows, as the grid was laid before the dungeons had rows of their own, and the dungeon chests are the digits 1 to 0. No key moved when the cells did. The bottles' contents and the capacities have no key until you bind one. The toolbar's **Shortcuts** button opens the dialog: **Use keyboard shortcuts** turns every key off at once, the table lists each control with its keys (activate one and press its new key; Delete removes it, Escape cancels), and **Reset to defaults** brings the original layout back. A key the browser or the system keeps for itself is refused. The desktop app adds a **Global** tab (see below).

**New game and key cells.** **New game** asks first, then clears what you found and checked, keeping the run type and its settings; **Undo** takes it back. **Show key cells**, a checkbox in the run type dialog (**Mode**) under the settings summary, shows each dungeon's big key and small keys under the grid, and its map and compass in a run that moves those out of their dungeons. They show on their own when maps and compasses leave their dungeons (MC, MCS, Full Shuffle); in other runs, show them to track keys by hand (the key logic counts them). The choice is kept on the device.

**Presets.** Pick a run type to set the rules. When a run's dungeon items leave their dungeons (alttpr.com's MC, MCS and Full Shuffle, the last one keysanity), the grid shows each dungeon's map and compass beside its keys, and its counter counts the items those leave room for. Or start from a seed: paste its alttpr.com link (or hash) under **Start from a seed**, and the tracker reads its settings (world, goal, swords, crystals, dungeon items, item placement, item pool and functionality, accessibility, boss shuffle, glitches) from alttpr.com and offers the run type that holds them, saying a boss shuffle in it ("Standard (Standard, Ganon, 7/7, Full boss shuffle)"). A seed generated with glitches (Overworld Glitches, Hybrid Major Glitches, Major Glitches or No Logic) keeps them in its run, said in the run type ("Open (Open, Ganon, 7/7, Overworld Glitches)") and under **Glitches** in the summary: the tracker has no logic for them, so the map says so and shows every check as maybe rather than a NoGlitches answer that would be wrong. The note names the cause, and every one that applies: the glitched logic, an entrance shuffle other than Crosskeys' ("Full entrance shuffle"), Crosskeys with its keys kept in their dungeons or in the Inverted world, shuffled bosses behind Crossworld entrances. Autotracking reads the same from the ROM, which tells Overworld Glitches apart but names the other three Major Glitches; the seed's file, when alttpr.com is asked for it, names each. In Race Mode (set always on, or on while autotracking a race seed) it is off and says so: the file alttpr.com keeps for a seed holds where every item is. Basic placement, a hard item pool or functionality change what is in logic as the randomizer has it: Ganon wants the silver arrows, no mirror shield, a bottle refills half the magic. The crystals, item placement, pool, functionality, accessibility and boss shuffle change in the run type dialog's summary at any time, the run kept (one Undo takes a change back). Progress is saved to the browser automatically, and can be exported or imported as JSON.

## Super Metroid (Map Rando)

Pick **Super Metroid** under **Game** in the run type dialog (Mode), where the other games are too. Each game keeps its own run on the device: switching and coming back finds it as it was, and the page loads only the game you track. The tracker follows **Map Rando** (maprando.com), the randomizer the community races: about 99 % of racetime.gg's Super Metroid Randomizer races of August to October 2026 were Map Rando seeds.

- **Items**: the beams, the morph ball and suits, the movement items, and how many missile, super missile and power bomb packs and energy and reserve tanks you hold. Click a cell to add one, right-click or "-" to take one away; a cell greys while you hold none.
- **The seed's objectives**: pick its preset in the run type dialog (Bosses, Minibosses, Chozos, Pirates, Metroids, None) or, for a Random seed, tick the ones its pause screen lists. Tick each as you finish it; once all are done, the barriers before Mother Brain are down.
- **The items a seed adds**: the Wall Jump Boots and the split Speed Booster (the Spark and Blue Boosters) take their cells when you turn them on.
- **No map and no logic**: Map Rando reconnects the game's rooms, so follow the game's own map. Export, Import, Undo, Redo and the stream view work as for A Link to the Past: Export and Share are off while Race Mode is on, which it is until you turn it **Off** in the Autotracking dialog (Map Rando's ROMs carry no race flag); in the desktop app Share stays on for live follow, as in a race of any game, and offers no link.
- **Run timer**, above the grid at the right: click it to start, pause, resume or reset it, as A Link to the Past's; **End run** stops it for good, and once it is stopped **Review run** beside it compares the run with the seed's spoiler log ([Reviewing a run](#reviewing-a-run)). While autotracking reads your game outside Race Mode it starts on its own at the first reading in play after the title, the file select or the loading of a game (not when you connect to a game already running or reset while it plays, where the panel tells you to start it yourself, nor after a pause, a door or a death) and stops for good when Samus is in her ship after Mother Brain, even a run whose timer never started: untick **Start and stop with the game** to keep it yours. In Race Mode it is yours alone (start it, and **End run** at the end): no Map Rando race rule speaks of a tracker driving it, and racetime.gg times the race. A new game, an import and Reset clear it; it is saved with the run, but never in an export or a shared link. The **Clock** choice picks what it counts. **Real time** (the default) is the time that passes, as races time it. **Game time** is Map Rando's own RTA clock, the one its end credits print as the final time: every frame counted, lag, menus and the file select included, at the SNES's 60.0988 frames a second. That clock counts since the console's save memory was set up, so the tracker notes it when the timer starts and shows the time since. Outside Race Mode, while autotracking reads it, the timer is set on it at each reading and ticks between two; it stops while the counter stands still (a paused emulator) and at the goal, and the game then drives it, so Start, Pause and the checkbox are off. A timer you started by hand goes on from what it shows. In Race Mode the tracker never reads that clock (a race rule of its own: the game shows it only in its credits), so the timer counts real time and the panel says why (the game does not start or stop it then either, as said above). The stream view's timer (`&timer=1`) follows whichever time the timer shows, and so does the overlay API outside Race Mode (in it, the API tells no time).
- **Autotracking**: connected as for A Link to the Past (below), the tracker recognises a Map Rando ROM and follows it: the items you hold (a pack or tank counts from the maximum it raised, so the seed's own pack sizes count right, and an energy tank you disabled from the pause menu still counts), the seed's objectives from its ROM and those done, and whether it adds the Wall Jump Boots or the split Speed Booster. **What I read** names the seed, linked to its maprando.com page, and its settings presets. racetime.gg's Super Metroid Randomizer category, where Map Rando is raced, says nothing of autotracking; the one rule found, a Map Rando tournament's, allows it "for items and boss kills" and never for the map layout, which the tracker never reads. Map Rando's ROM has no race flag, so **Race Mode is on by default** (its option reads **On** or **Off**; turn it off outside a race; a device that saved Auto before this version reads it as the default, On, and Off once chosen stays). Its badge reads **Race Mode**, claiming no legality. In Race Mode the tracker reads the items you hold and the bosses you beat, and nothing else: set the seed's objectives, the Wall Jump Boots and the split Speed Booster yourself in **Mode**, and the board marks done the objectives of yours a boss kill completes (the four bosses, the minibosses, Bomb Torizo), leaving a statue used or a room cleared of its pirates or metroids to you; the run timer is yours to start and end; the game's clocks and the places you took are never read, and **What I read** lists the bosses beaten, with no seed. Outside Race Mode it also notes which of the 100 item locations you took, with the run timer's time then, which a review reads. Like A Link to the Past's, it changes a cell only when the game does and never undoes a mark you set beyond it, and lays nothing outside a game in play (the title screen still holds the last file's items).

## SMZ3 (samus.link)

Pick **SMZ3** under **Game** in the run type dialog. The tracker follows **samus.link**, the randomizer the community races SMZ3 with: 456 of racetime.gg's 475 SMZ3 races of 2025 and 2026 were samus.link seeds, its beta included.

- **Items**: both games' items. Click a cell to add one, right-click or "-" to take one away; the sword, shield, gloves and bottles show their level on the sprite, the packs and tanks their count. Under them, the pendants and crystals found, and Super Metroid's boss tokens with how many Tourian asks for.
- **Two maps, one logic**: Hyrule (its two worlds, as for A Link to the Past) and Zebes. Every location takes the state SMZ3's own logic gives it, ported from the randomizer's source (`tewtal/SMZ3Randomizer`) and held to its official logic tests: available, maybe (it depends on what the tracker cannot know yet, such as a medallion or where an item sits), or out of logic. Click a marker to check its locations.
- **Run type**: a preset (Normal 7/7/4, the weeklies' Normal 7/5/3, Hard, Normal keysanity) or each setting: Super Metroid's logic (Normal or Hard), keysanity, the crystals Ganon's Tower and Ganon ask for, and the bosses Tourian asks for. Set Misery Mire's and Turtle Rock's medallions once you know them, and their dungeons' checks stop being maybes.
- **Keysanity**: each dungeon's big key and small keys take two rows of the grid, as on A Link to the Past's keysanity board, and Super Metroid's keycards a table under the tokens; the logic reads them.
- **Run timer**, above the board at the right: click it to start, pause, resume or reset it, as Super Metroid's. While autotracking reads your game it starts on its own at the first reading in play after the title, the file select or the loading of a game, in whichever game your file starts in (not when you connect to a game already running or reset while it plays, where the panel tells you to start it yourself, nor after a pause, a door, a portal or a death), and stops for good when the credits run, which only happens once both games' endings are reached, even a run whose timer never started: untick **Start and stop with the game** to keep it yours. A new game, an import and Reset clear it; it is saved with the run, but never in an export or a shared link. The **Clock** choice picks what it counts. **Real time** (the default) is the time that passes, as races time it. **Game time** is the games' own frame counters added up, the sum the credits print as TOTAL TIME: Super Metroid's NMI count and Zelda's, the running game's live and the other's as it saved it before the last portal, at 60 frames a second as the credits count them, so the timer ends at the time the game shows. Outside Race Mode, while autotracking reads them, the timer is set on that sum at each reading and ticks between two; it stops while the sum stands still (a paused emulator) and at the credits, and the game then drives it, so Start, Pause and the checkbox are off. Like the credits' own total, the sum goes back to your last save when you die or reload, in both games, and the timer goes back with it: it shows the game's time, which does not count what you replayed. In Race Mode (a race seed, or Race Mode set always on) the tracker never reads the counters (a race rule of its own: the game shows its time only in its credits), so the timer counts real time and the panel says why; starting and stopping with the game still work, as they read only the game's state. The stream view's timer (`&timer=1`) follows whichever time the timer shows, and so does the overlay API outside Race Mode (in it, the API tells no time).
- **Autotracking**: connected as for A Link to the Past (below), the tracker follows your SMZ3 game in both games, whichever one you are in: the items you hold, the rewards and boss tokens found, the keys and keycards, the locations checked on both maps, and the seed's Super Metroid logic and keysanity. Outside races it reads the seed's requirements (the crystals Ganon's Tower and Ganon ask for, the bosses Tourian asks for) and the medallions Misery Mire and Turtle Rock want, where the emulator serves that part of the ROM: RetroArch with bsnes-mercury does not, and the dialog says so rather than guess. In Race Mode (a race seed, or Race Mode set always on) it never does, nor does it read the run timer's game counters: SMZ3 has no autotracking specification, and racetime.gg allows autotracking but bans inspecting the ROM otherwise, so the tracker reads only what you see in the game. SMZ3's tournaments race under A Link to the Past's autotracking rules (v2.0), which forbid telling a 5/6 crystal from another: in Race Mode the crystals, red and blue, stay yours to mark. Which dungeon or boss holds which reward is never read, race or not: the pendants, crystals and boss tokens you hold are, so a reward counts once found. **What I read** names the seed (outside Race Mode), which game runs and what was read. Like A Link to the Past's, it changes a value only when the game does, never undoes a mark you set beyond it, and lays nothing outside a game in play.
- **Review**: once the run is over, the seed's spoiler log set against it (see [Reviewing a run](#reviewing-a-run)).
- Export, Import, Undo, Redo and the stream view work as for the other games, Export and Share being off while Race Mode is on or not yet known to be off.

## Archipelago

Pick **Archipelago** under **Game** in the run type dialog, or open [the tracker on it](https://kqesar.github.io/alltp-tracker/?game=archipelago). [Archipelago](https://archipelago.gg) is a multiworld randomizer of its own: you play your game with Archipelago's own client (SNIClient for A Link to the Past), which sends your checks and receives your items. The board joins the same room as a **tracker on your slot**: it reads what the room's server says and can send nothing. It works for any game of the room, from any device, a phone included.

- **Connect**: the **Room** dialog opens on its own the first time, and from the toolbar's **Room** button. Paste the room's address (`archipelago.gg:38281`), the `/connect` line its page shows, or the page's link (`https://archipelago.gg/room/…`): a link fills the port and lists the room's slots to pick from. Type your slot name exactly as the room lists it (case matters), and the password if the room has one. The password stays in the tab, or on the device if you tick **Remember on this device**; it is never shared, exported or put in a report. The steps under the form tick themselves as the connection goes, and say what to fix when one fails.
- **The room sees the tracker.** Everyone in the room sees it join and leave ("… tracking … has joined"). Events set their own rules on trackers: check them before a race.
- **The room line**: your slot, its game and team, how many players, a **Race seed** badge when the room was generated as a race, and the connection's state. It never shows the room's address.
- **Items**: everything your slot received. A Link to the Past, Super Metroid and SMZ3 are drawn as grids with the tracker's art, built for Archipelago's own items: swords, gloves, bows and bottles at their levels, each dungeon's keys, Super Metroid's beams, suits and boots, its tanks and ammunition counted. SMZ3's grid adds a row for the keycards and Super Metroid's maps once a keysanity seed sends the first one. What has no cell (capacity upgrades, Triforce pieces, rupees, hearts, dungeon maps and compasses) is listed under **More items**, by the names the room gives them. Any other game is a list, by Archipelago's kinds: progression, useful, filler, traps.
- **Checks**: your slot's locations, by the game's own groups (for Super Metroid, which names none, by area: Crateria and Blue Brinstar, Kraid's Lair…; for SMZ3, by region: Light World South, Brinstar Pink…), with how many are left, a filter and **Hide checked**. Each group opens and closes ("Ice Palace — 11 left / 12"): those with checks left start open beside the items, every one starts closed on a phone or a narrow window, and a filter opens every group it matches. Under each location you checked, what it held and for whom ("E Tank for Hybrid Hero"). Archipelago's server says what you checked. It does not say what you can reach: the board has no logic.
- **Feed**, under the items: the room as Archipelago's own text client prints it, newest first. Items sent and found ("Quester sent Bombs (3) to Link (Top Middle Chest)"), hints, goals, a release or collect as one line ("Quester released 143 items, 12 for you"), players joining and leaving, the countdown. No chat. **Involving me** (the default) keeps your items, hints, goal and the countdown; **Whole room** shows everything. The feed is a log a screen reader does not read aloud as it grows; tick **Announce items I receive** to hear each progression item that reaches you.
- **Hints** (beside the checks): the items hinted for you and where they sit, then others' items hinted in your world, most wanted first, each status in words (Priority, Unspecified, No priority, Avoid, Found), with your hint points and what a hint costs. The board never asks for a hint: ask one from your game's client (`!hint`).
- **Players**: each slot's name, game and where its game stands (not connected, connected, ready, playing, goal). Outside a race, your slot's settings as the room sends them, for information.
- **While you were away**: after a reload, a drop or a closed laptop, a notice lists the items you received meanwhile, who sent each and from where.
- **Run timer**, in the room line: it starts on its own when the room's countdown (`!countdown`) reaches GO, and stops for good when your game reaches its goal (the room says so as it happens), unless you untick **Start at the room's countdown, stop at your goal**; start, pause and reset it yourself otherwise. A reload after the goal shows the time it stopped at.
- **Nothing to click on the board**: it is the server's. No undo, New game, Export, Import or Share; open the room again instead.
- **Drops and reloads**: the board reconnects on its own (at once when the device comes back online or the tab back into view). A reload shows the last board at once, then connects again. An archipelago.gg room idle for hours is paused: the dialog links to its page, which wakes it.
- **An Archipelago ROM on the other boards**: autotracking on the A Link to the Past, Super Metroid or SMZ3 board knows a ROM Archipelago patched by its title, says so, and offers **Open the Archipelago board**: Archipelago's own client reads that game, and the board follows your slot from the room.
- **Tabs and devices**: one tab of the browser connects; another says "Connected in another tab", follows it, and takes over when it closes. Another device can connect too, or follow the desktop app's board live.
- **Join links**: **Copy a join link**, in the Room dialog, copies a link that opens the board with the room and your slot filled in, for your phone or a teammate. It never holds the password, and it never connects on its own: the dialog opens, you add the password if the room has one, then Connect.
- **Where it works**: archipelago.gg rooms work everywhere. A room someone hosts themselves depends on where it runs and on your browser; the dialog says what will happen as soon as you type the address, before anything connects:

  | The room's server | Chrome, Edge | Firefox | Safari, iPhone, iPad | Desktop app |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | archipelago.gg | yes | yes | yes | yes |
  | `wss://` (with TLS), anywhere | yes¹ | yes | yes² | yes |
  | `ws://` on this computer (`localhost`) | yes¹ | yes¹ | no | yes |
  | `ws://` on your network (`192.168.…`) | yes¹ | no | no | yes³ |
  | `ws://` on the internet | no | no | no | yes³ |

  ¹ The first time, the browser may ask whether the site may reach apps on this device, or devices on your network: choose **Allow**. Blocked by mistake? Click the icon left of the address and set **Apps on device** (this computer) or **Local network** (your network) to Allow; the tracker connects as soon as you do. ² Not tried yet. ³ Tried on Linux; not yet on Windows or macOS. A host who can add TLS (`wss://`) lets every browser in; otherwise use the [desktop app](#the-desktop-app).
- **Stream view**: the item grid (A Link to the Past, Super Metroid, SMZ3), or the progression items you hold, a line each, for any other game. In the **Stream view** dialog (the desktop app's Stream tools too), tick **Show the run timer under the grid** (`&timer=1`) for the run timer, drawn as for the other games, and **Show the room's last 5 lines involving you** (`&feed=1`) for the feed's last five lines involving you under it: never a hint, never the room's address. Both are off unless you ask.

## Autotracking

The tracker can read your game as you play, through [SNI](https://github.com/alttpo/sni/releases/latest) (or QUsb2Snes) running on your computer: RetroArch with the bsnes-mercury core, an FX Pak Pro, BizHawk or an NWA emulator, on Windows, macOS or Linux. The toolbar's **Autotracking** button opens the setup guide for your system, its steps ticked as the connection gets through them, the failing one opened with what to do. Its dot shows the status at a glance; connecting, losing SNI and reconnecting never interrupt the run with a dialog. Once connected, the tracker connects again each time it opens, on that device, until you disconnect. With OBS, autotrack in the dock: the stream view follows the dock and never connects to SNI itself. Only one tab of the tracker reads the game: open a second and it waits, following the board, and takes over when the first closes.

**The board follows the game.** As you play, the tracker marks what the game shows: items, bosses beaten, checked locations, the chests each dungeon has left, the dungeons' prizes, and with small keys outside their dungeons (MCS and Full Shuffle) the small keys found, as the game counts them: a key spent on a door still counts, and so does one found while the tracker was closed, while a key an enemy or a pot drops does not (the logic never counts those). A board saved by an earlier version may hold more, counted from the keys in hand, drops included: **Sync from game** sets it to the game's count. In a retro run, whose keys open any dungeon, no key count is read. Outside Race Mode the tracker shows a dungeon's prize when the game would show it to you: once the dungeon is finished, or as soon as the world map draws it in the world you are in (from the start in most seeds; with maps outside their dungeons, once you hold the dungeon's map or Sahasrahla or the Bomb Merchant has marked it), never earlier. It reads which prize each dungeon holds from the seed's ROM, and keeps only those the game shows; an emulator that does not serve the ROM leaves a finished dungeon's prize to be told once only one fits. A prize you named yourself stays yours. Likewise the medallion Misery Mire and Turtle Rock ask for: the game shows it only as the emblem carved at the dungeon's entrance, so the tracker fills it in (top right of the boss cell) once you are on that entrance's overworld screen, or when the entrance is open, never before, and keeps it; one you set yourself stays yours, and **Sync from game** sets it to the seed's. It changes a value only when the game does, and never undoes a mark you set beyond the game, such as a prize you saw on the map before beating its boss. A run replaced while the game is read (**New game**, an **Import**, a shared link, a new run type) is the exception: the game is laid on the new board at once, as at the first reading, under the same Race Mode, and what the tracker kept from the earlier run (the pad medallions shown, the big bomb bought) is forgotten, as it is when the game starts another seed. **Sync from game**, in the Autotracking dialog, sets the board to the game, clearing such marks; Undo takes it back. What the game marks is never a step of Undo: undoing your own change keeps the game's. In a Crosskeys run, it notes each door or hole you go through, and the door you come out by: where the seed's ROM says it leads, so the map and the logic follow you without a click. A door the game shows overrides your note for it. Memory keeps only the last door each way, so a door you take while the tracker is closed is not caught up. While small keys stay in their dungeons, the game's count of keys found tells how many of the chests opened held them, so a dungeon's counter counts its items only. Outside Race Mode the logic also follows what no cell shows: the flute once you activate it (in Inverted, flying then needs nothing more), Standard's escape (the Light World stays shut until Zelda is in the Sanctuary; when autotracking stops or Race Mode comes on, the escape counts done again, as by hand), and in Inverted the Bomb Merchant, checked once you carry the big bomb out of his shop, which the game keeps no other trace of. A shield a Pikit takes stays marked until **Sync from game** (the logic only ever asks for the mirror shield, which a Pikit never takes). **What I read** lists everything the tracker read, to compare with your game: the dungeon prizes it shows, each with why (dungeon finished, its map held, on the world map, marked by Sahasrahla or the Bomb Merchant), the pad medallions it shows, and what it read that the board has no cell for (heart pieces…).

**Dark room map.** In a dark room without the Lamp, the map panel shows the room above its tabs and where you are in it. By default it is the room as the game would draw it lit, rebuilt from your own console and ROM (the tracker carries none of the game's graphics), with you as the game draws you: your pose, the way you face, your sword and shield. Unopened chests are ringed in gold, the nearest one pulsing, and while you hold the sword a ring around you is dashed as it charges and whole once a spin attack is ready. While the graphics load, the plan shows; when they cannot be had (another base patch, a read that failed, which is tried again a few times, or a console that cannot serve them), it stays, with a line under it saying why. The plan is the game's own collision map, drawn from above, with a ring and an arrow for you: walls, floor, pits, water, spikes, stairs, doorways and the exit, closed doors (a key door, a bombable wall, a shutter), pots and blocks, torches (lit or not) and chests (closed or opened) each have a shape of their own, named in a legend below it, so no colour stands alone. A line under the picture says where the nearest chest you have not opened is, in tiles ("7 tiles right, 3 tiles down"), how many torches are not lit, which way you face and whether your sword is charged, and a screen reader hears it once it has settled, not at every step. It appears when the game is in normal play in a dungeon, the room is dark and you hold no Lamp, and goes the moment the room is lit, you find the Lamp, you leave the dungeon or you disconnect; a menu or a text box over the room leaves it as it is. **Dark room map** in the Autotracking dialog, on by default and kept on the device, turns it off, and then none of its memory is read; **Dark room map style** under it picks **Game art** or **Plan** (the plan reads less: none of the picture's memory or ROM). It is an assist the game keeps from you without the Lamp, so it is **never shown in Race Mode, and never read there**. It lives in the page's memory only: it is not saved, exported, shared, streamed, followed or part of the overlay API. See [the pack's notes](../packs/alttpr/README.md#the-dark-room-map) for what is read, and [the game's graphics](../packs/alttpr/README.md#the-dark-room-in-the-games-graphics) for how the picture is rebuilt and what it leaves out (other sprites, such as enemies and keys on the floor, are not drawn).

**The seed sets the run type.** The tracker reads the seed's settings from its ROM (the world, swords, goal, crystals, which dungeon items leave their dungeons), and, outside Race Mode, from the file alttpr.com keeps for the seed what no ROM states (item placement, item pool and functionality, accessibility, boss shuffle, and whether the entrances are shuffled, as in Crosskeys), and holds them against the board's run type, once the file has come or could not. On a board with nothing marked yet it switches to the seed's on its own, and says so. With a run on the board it asks instead: **Switch** starts the seed's run type with the game laid on it (one Undo brings your board back), **Keep mine** keeps yours for that seed, on later visits too. The Autotracking dialog says what the seed's settings mean for the run type, and **What I read** lists them. A seed no run type here holds (the Ganonhunt goal, only some dungeon items shuffled) is said, never rounded.

Races: racetime.gg's ALTTPR category allows autotracking under the [ALTTPR Auto-tracker Rules and Guidelines v2.0](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vTgsj0FMiWyaBOYP97e_ABc4P_0vIGiJxoUJ5Rj-PBr1T7i_zpyLqhLLVkbftPwS_B6nwad5j5zhtMg/pub). On a race seed, or when its race flag cannot be read, the tracker turns on Race Mode, which keeps to those rules, and shows a **race-legal** badge. Racing a seed generated without the race flag (a practice race, a seed made without "race")? Set **Race Mode** to **Always on** in the Autotracking dialog (it is **Auto (the ROM's race flag)** by default): the tracker then behaves exactly as on a race seed, and the badge reads **Race-legal | Always on**. The choice is saved on the device, for each game, and applies at once, even mid-session. Race Mode reads what v2.0 permits in so many words, and nothing it leaves in doubt: your items and dungeon items, the bosses beaten, the places you checked, inside the dungeons too, and each dungeon's chests left (v2.0: "overworld-locations, underworld-locations, and dungeon-locations"), the hearts on screen, the heart pieces toward the next one, the Triforce pieces of a Triforce Hunt (v2.0 lists them among the items) and your bottles and what each holds ("heart count, heart piece count, and bottle contents"), and the pendants and crystals you took ("their prizes upon collection (but not identify if a crystal is a 5/6 crystal or a regular crystal)"): the pendants by name, the crystals as a count, which the logic reads as regular crystals until you hold more crystals than regular ones. The flute counts as found, never as activated: in Inverted, where the bird must be woken, flying reads maybe until you could have woken it in logic. It never says which crystals are 5 and 6, nor which dungeon gave which prize (v2.0 forbids tying an item to a location): a dungeon's prize, red crystals included, stays yours to mark, and so does where entrances lead (v2.0 forbids tracking it), Sync from game included; the ROM's settings and its own stats are not read, nor are the bomb count, the bomb and arrow capacities and the stats' heart counters, and Race Mode never says whether Zelda was rescued (v2.0 forbids it in a Standard seed): **What I read** leaves it out, and the logic counts the escape done from the start, as it does by hand. Race Mode never reads Link's position, room or dungeon, never switches maps or tracks entrances on its own, and the dark room map stays off, as the race admins' clarification of June 2026 (no change to v2.0) asks of a tracker in a race; you stay responsible for your tools. Door Rando, Overworld Rando and tournaments may amend these rules for their races: the tracker keeps to v2.0 as written. Super Metroid's and SMZ3's racetime.gg categories link no tracker rules: their Race Mode is as their sections above say, held to what their communities' tournament rules allow. In Race Mode the tracker reads no setting from the ROM, not even the logic (v2.0 lets a tracker set itself up from them only when it can ensure the seed is no Mystery one, which a race seed's ROM does not say), **What I read** names no seed (v2.0 lets a tracker show only what it lists before the seed is completed), and it asks alttpr.com for nothing: the file alttpr.com keeps for a seed holds its patch, where every item is, and the tracker keeps clear of it during a race. Pick the run type yourself (Mystery included); **Start from a seed** stays off while Race Mode is on. The Run tab's timeline then lists the items you found, not the places you checked. A saved file and a shared link hold the places you checked and when, so **Export** and **Share** are off too, for every game, while Race Mode is on or autotracking has not yet read whether it is (Auto, connecting or set to connect on start, nothing read): the buttons stay, greyed, in the toolbar or, where it folds them away, in ALttPR's More menu, with the reason as their description; activating one only says the reason in the status line (every time, so a screen reader hears it again). In the desktop app **Share** stays on in a race, for live follow: its dialog then says no link is made, and shows one as soon as Race Mode goes off while it is open. They come back the moment Race Mode is off, with no reload. **Import** stays, since it sends nothing. A race is remembered: once autotracking has read the seed as a race's (the ROM's race flag says race, or is one it cannot read or does not know: in doubt, a race; a flag that says open never counts), or a session ended in Race Mode before the flag was read, Race Mode stays on for that run through a **Disconnect**, the end of the session and a reload, so that autotracking (which then decodes nothing a race keeps back, whatever a ROM's flag says), Export, Share, the overlay API, live follow, the Run tab's timeline, **Start from a seed** and **Review run** keep to it in the middle of the race (not even a later open flag undoes it). It ends when the run is replaced, by **New game**, an **Import** or a shared link opened into the tracker, or when you say the race is over. **Undo** after one of those brings the race back with its board, so the board you get back is never one that can be exported or shared. While only the memory holds Race Mode, the Autotracking button and dialog show a **Race Mode** badge, the dialog's Race Mode section says why it is on, and its **The race is over** button turns it off for this run. It applies to A Link to the Past and SMZ3, which have a race flag; Super Metroid has none and is unchanged. Race Mode held always on is yours to turn off in the dialog, as before.

Safari, and every browser on iOS, cannot reach SNI from this secure site (it counts `ws://localhost` as mixed content): use Firefox, Chrome or Edge, or the [desktop app](#the-desktop-app), which reaches SNI from any Mac. The first time, Firefox and Chrome ask whether the site may reach apps on your device: allow it. SNI listening on another port than 23074? Add `?bridge=<port>` to the tracker's address.

## Voice commands

Mark what you find without leaving the game: say **"tracker"**, then the find. For example, "tracker hook shot", "tracker master sword", "tracker eastern boss", "tracker mire ether", "tracker eastern crystal" or "tracker undo". Open **Voice** beside the run timer and choose **Start listening**.

- **What a command does.** It says what you found, not a click:
  - saying the hookshot again keeps it;
  - "sword" is one level up and never wraps round to none;
  - "half magic" marks the ½ cell, and keeps a quarter magic the game already gave;
  - "eastern chest" takes one item from the count.
  - Each command is one step of Undo, as a click is. The Voice button shows it for a moment, and a screen reader hears it.
- **Talking is safe.** Without "tracker" first, nothing is marked, so talking about an item on stream changes nothing.
- **Private.** Speech is recognized in your browser, by [Vosk](https://alphacephei.com/vosk/), and no sound leaves your computer.
  - The first start downloads the speech model, 40 MB, kept for next time (about 60 MB of the browser's storage; **Remove the speech model** in the dialog frees it).
  - Nothing is downloaded until you turn voice commands on.
  - Recognition runs in a Web Worker, so it does not block the page.
- **Words the model knows.** It is English only, and its dictionary lacks a few of the game's names, so they are said as words it has. The dialog's **What to say** lists every command:
  - "bom boss" for Bombos (saying "bombos" works);
  - "samaria" for Somaria;
  - "burner" or "blue cane" for Byrna;
  - "aga" for Agahnim;
  - "gannon tower" or "g t" for Ganon's Tower.
- **Where it works.** In Firefox, Chrome and Edge, in a browser tab: an OBS dock has no microphone. Once on, the tracker listens again each time it opens, on that device, until you stop it. Only one tab listens at a time.

## Sharing a run

The toolbar's **Share** button copies a link that holds your run, whatever the game, and opens a **Share this run** dialog: it says how the copy went, shows the link as a QR code (scan it to open the run on a phone or another device; it is a snapshot that does not follow the game) and has a **Copy the snapshot link** button to copy it again. If your browser refuses the clipboard, the QR code is still there. The run is compressed into the address's fragment (`#run=…`), which the browser never sends to any server. Whoever opens it is asked "Open this shared run?" and chooses **Open** (loaded as an Import is, **Undo** takes it back) or **Keep mine**; nothing is replaced without that answer. A very long ALttPR run (past about 300 finds autotracked on the game's clock) is too long for a Discord message with its timeline, so the link then leaves the timeline out and says so; the dialog's **Copy the full link (with the timeline)** still copies the whole one, its length said, for a review of the run on another device. **Share** is off while Race Mode is on, or not yet known to be off, as **Export** is: a link holds the places you checked and when (see [Races](#autotracking)). In the desktop app **Share** stays on in a race for live follow, below, and its dialog says no link is made. [Sharing a run through its URL](share.md) has the format and the measured lengths.

## Reviewing a run

Once a run is over you can import the seed's spoiler log and see how the run went against it. A review is for a **finished** run: the timer stopped at the goal, or stopped by **End run** in the timer's panel. It never opens in **Race Mode** (a race keeps its spoiler closed), and a run still counting or paused has none, so a review cannot spoil a run in progress or a stream of it.

Save the log from the seed's page (alttpr.com: **Save Spoiler**, which the seed page shows once you have loaded your base ROM there with **Select ROM File**; maprando.com: the seed page's **Spoiler log JSON**; samus.link: **Download** on the spoiler), then use **Review run** beside the timer (the only way on Super Metroid's and SMZ3's boards), or **Import the spoiler log…** in the Run tab, and pick the file. It is read on your device and never sent anywhere. The tracker checks it is this run's: the seed in the file's name (or the seed file's hash) against the run's own seed, and the settings, medallions and prizes both state. A file of another seed or other settings is refused with the reason. When nothing proves it (a run tracked by hand, a renamed file) the review opens marked **Not verified**.

A review lives in the page's memory only: it is not saved, exported, shared, streamed or followed, and reloading the page, **Reset**, **New game**, importing or sharing another run, switching seed or turning Race Mode on closes it. **Close review** closes it too.

**A Link to the Past.** The Run tab's **Review** section says, for each item the seed needed, when the items you held first put its place in logic and when you took it: how long it **waited** for you. It replays your run's timeline through the randomizer's rules, with each dungeon key where the log put it, so it works for a run autotracking followed.
- **The tiles**: your play time (the timer's, less each stretch of 30 minutes or more where nothing was found, said as breaks), the needed items you took, and the longest wait.
- **What waited for you the longest**: the five longest, each with where it was, **In logic from** (and after which item), **Taken** and **Waited**, drawn on the run's play time (waiting hatched, taken a dot, breaks dotted). For an item in a dungeon only part of which was in logic, how many of its places were and when the whole dungeon was, and when you entered it. **Trade-off** says what holding it earlier could have opened, and from when: a choice, not a fault (skipping a dungeon only partly in logic is a fair route). **Show all** lists every item that waited, dungeon items and prizes included.
- **The chain behind the run**: the items each in logic only once you had the one before.
- **Dungeons**: when part of each, then all of it, was in logic, when you entered and when its boss fell.
- **Before the logic expected**: items you took before the items you held put them in logic, or whose place was not in logic by the run's end, without guessing why.
- **Places that mattered, never checked**, grouped (a dungeon left mostly unchecked is one line), and **Details by step**, the seed's playthrough folded, with each item's times. **How these times are worked out** says what is exact and what is an estimate (the breaks, which copy of a sword or glove was which, an item with no place, a key whose place you never checked).
- **When it cannot tell**, one sentence says why and the playthrough shows with the timer's readings instead: small keys or entrances shuffled (the run does not time them), settings hidden (Mystery), bosses shuffled or a glitched logic, a run autotracked in Race Mode by an earlier version of the tracker (which did not read which dungeon chest you opened), a run tracked by hand (its times are clicks), or no times at all. A run that came without its timeline (opened from a shared link that left it out to stay short) says so first: review it on the device where it was played, or open its full link (**Copy the full link (with the timeline)** in the Share dialog). A dungeon's prize counts as taken once its boss is beaten, timed by the boss when the run has that time.

Then the seed's medallions, prizes and starting items. The timeline names what each checked location held. **On the board**, a review marks each map marker with the item it held (a small image on its corner, the most notable one for a cave with several), with a solid ring when everything there was checked and a dashed one when not, and says it in the marker's name ("held Bug Catching Net, Magic Powder and 3 more; not all checked"). The Checks tab names the item after each check and gains **Missed only**, which lists every check left undone whatever the state filters say. A grid cell's name says where its item was ("at Zora's Domain", or how many places for a progressive one), and its tooltip lists them. **Close review** in the banner over the tabs puts the board back as it was. A dungeon's checks that the board only counts (a run tracked by hand) are listed as chests left, not as missed. Spoiler races' logs (placements without a playthrough) and entrance-shuffle logs are read; the real connections of a shuffle are not shown yet.

**Super Metroid.** Its Race Mode is on by default: turn it **Off** in the Autotracking dialog once the race is over to review the run. Map Rando's log (about 21 MB, read in the background without freezing the page) never names its seed, so a review is always **Not verified**: it is refused only when what it states contradicts the run, the seed's objectives or the Wall Jump Boots and split Speed Booster it places (a run tracked by hand that never changed them says nothing, so nothing contradicts). The **Review** section under the board gives, in the playthrough's order, where each single item was (its room, node and area, and the **step** of the playthrough where it becomes reachable), those your board never held first, then how many of each tank and pack you hold against those the seed placed, with every one's place by area, and the seed's start, objectives and pack sizes. Each grid cell says where its item was (the cell's name and tooltip). When autotracking followed your game, the review also goes **place by place**: each single item's place says **Found at 12:34** (the run timer's time when the tracker saw you take it, or just **Found** when the timer had not started) or **Not found**, and the tanks and packs count the places you found, each listed with its own time; a place that was already taken when autotracking connected mid-run is dated by that moment. If your board holds an item whose place autotracking did not see (Race Mode was on for part of the run, or you marked it by hand), the review falls back to the count and says so. A run you tracked by hand, or in Race Mode (turn it on for a race: it never reads which places you took), keeps the count review: the board holds items, so it cannot say which location you took an item from, nor when.

**SMZ3.** The seed is checked by its guid (the one the game states while autotracking reads it, outside Race Mode), and by the logic, keysanity, crystal and boss counts and the medallions you set; a hand-tracked run, which knows no guid, opens as "Not verified" with how many facts agreed. The file is the Download's text or the JSON samus.link's page reads (`/api/spoiler/<guid>`). A **Review** tab joins Hyrule and Zebes under the board. SMZ3's board keeps no times, so it says what you checked, not when: how many of the seed's places you checked, the playthrough sphere by sphere (a sphere is how soon the seed's logic reaches a place) with each place's item and whether you checked it, the places not checked by region with what they held, the seed's medallions and rewards. The Hyrule and Zebes tabs draw the item each marker held on it (a solid ring for a place you checked, a dashed one for one you did not), name it for a screen reader, and the grid says where the seed put each item.

## Streaming with OBS

The stream view, [`?view=obs`](https://kqesar.github.io/alltp-tracker/?view=obs), is the items alone on a transparent background, laid out as a band twelve cells wide that fits along an edge of the scene. It has three rows of items (the equipment, the Y items, then the bottles and the crystals held and asked for) and a row of dungeons from Castle Tower to Ganon's Tower, each with its key cell under it while those show. The pendants are left out, since each dungeon's prize shows them. On a page taller than wide, such as a phone following the run, the band folds into two halves of six (the second seven wide while the key cells show, Hyrule Castle's at its end). It fills its source, centred, at a size that keeps the pixel art sharp, and follows the run live. The toolbar's **Stream view** button copies its address (the desktop app serves it to OBS itself, [below](#the-desktop-app)). In OBS 31 or newer:

1. **Track in a dock**: under *Docks → Custom Browser Docks*, add `https://kqesar.github.io/alltp-tracker/` and track your run there.
2. **Show the grid**: add a *Browser Source* with the stream view's address. At 768 × 256 every icon pixel is drawn whole. With the key cells shown, use 832 × 320: a row for the keys, and a column at the end for Hyrule Castle's. Double it for a larger band, or pick any size. Set the size in the source's properties: dragging its handles resamples the image and blurs it.
3. **Check the sync on your first run**: the dock and the source share OBS's browser storage, so the source is expected to follow every click in the dock. Items can also be clicked through the source's *Interact* window.

**Keep a scene sized for the grid**: before the band, the stream view drew the tracker's grid, in a 512 × 448 source. Tick *Lay it out as the tracker's grid* in the **Stream view** dialog, and the address ends in `&layout=grid`. The view then draws the tracker's own grid, seven cells between two half cells, whole-pixel at 512 × 448. With the key cells shown it needs 512 × 576, two rows more. The timer, a Retro run's shops and a hunt's pieces take a row each (64 px more).

**Show the run timer**: tick *Show the run timer under the grid* in the **Stream view** dialog, and the address ends in `&timer=1`: the run timer sits under the grid, drawn in the chest counters' pixel font, white while it runs, grey while paused, gold once the goal stopped it. It takes a row of its own, so the sizes become 768 × 320 (832 × 384 with the key cells); Super Metroid's, six columns wide, 384 × 320 (four rows) with it; SMZ3's, seven columns wide, 448 × 576 (eight rows; 448 × 704 with the key cells).

Capturing a browser window instead? Window capture has no transparency: add `&bg=00ff00` (any 3, 4, 6 or 8-digit hex colour) for a solid background to key out with a *Chroma Key* filter. In a browser source, eight digits make a translucent backdrop instead, e.g. `&bg=000000a0`, for busy footage.

Items not yet obtained are greyed in the icon alone, as in the app, so they stay legible over bright and dark scenes alike. Nobody clicks in a browser source, so the stream view takes a new version of the tracker on its own as soon as it is ready.

## Offline and install

After its first visit, the tracker loads and works with no network at all: the browser keeps every file it needs (the code, the item art, the map), and the run stays saved on the device as always. It keeps the games you have opened and no others: a Super Metroid player downloads about 0.5 MB for offline use, an A Link to the Past player about 1.4 MB. Switch to another game while online once, and it works offline from then on; a game never opened online cannot start offline. To give it a window of its own, install it: the install button in the address bar of Chrome or Edge, or **Share → Add to Home Screen** in Safari on iOS.

Reloading the page (F5) while online always shows the latest version. A tracker left open checks for one every hour, when its tab comes back into view and when the network returns, and never reloads in the middle of a run: a notice, "A new version is available", waits for you. **Reload** switches to it and keeps your progress; dismissing it leaves the new version for the next time you open the tracker. A copy started offline is offered the new version too, once the browser has it.


## The desktop app

The tracker also comes as an app for Windows, macOS and Linux, from the [latest release](https://github.com/kqesar/alltp-tracker/releases/latest). It is the same tracker in a window of its own: it reads RetroArch itself, without SNI (turn on RetroArch's Network Commands and use the bsnes-mercury core, as the setup guide says), reaches SNI for an FX Pak Pro, BizHawk or an NWA emulator on every system, Safari's Macs included, and updates itself (a notice, "A new version is available", offers **Restart**, never in the middle of a run). It keeps its own run, apart from the browser's: **Export** (which asks where to save, as any desktop app does) and **Import** move a run between them. The installers still carry the project's former name, ALLTP Tracker.

| System | Download | The first time |
|---|---|---|
| Windows | `…_x64-setup.exe` | The app is not signed yet: Windows SmartScreen says "Windows protected your PC". Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**. |
| macOS | `…_aarch64.dmg` (Apple silicon) or `…_x64.dmg` (Intel) | The app is not notarized (that needs a paid Apple account): macOS refuses to open it once. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, choose **Open Anyway**, then **Open**. |
| Linux | `….AppImage` (any distribution), `….deb` or `….rpm` | An AppImage runs once made executable: `chmod +x` it, or tick "Allow executing" in its properties. It installs no menu entry, so on its first launch it offers to add itself to your application menu (**Add**; **Not now** is remembered). **More → Remove from application menu** takes it out again. |

**Global shortcuts.** Keys that step the tracker while the emulator has the focus, for A Link to the Past. Open **Shortcuts → Global**, turn on **Use global shortcuts** and bind the controls you want to reach from the game: nothing is bound by default, since a global key is taken from every app. Letters, digits and punctuation need two modifiers (on macOS, Ctrl or Cmd among them: Option types characters; on Windows, not Ctrl+Alt alone: that is AltGr). Numpad keys and F13 to F24 can stand alone, but a numpad key is then taken from every app, digits included, while the shortcuts are on. The tab says what your system does:

- **Windows, macOS, Linux on X11**: the key works as soon as it is bound. One held down steps once.
- **Linux on Wayland (KDE, GNOME 48+, Hyprland)**: your desktop asks you to approve the keys, when you close the dialog; it shows the keys it chose, and **Open shortcut settings** changes them. Tried on KDE Plasma (Wayland); GNOME 48+ and Hyprland are built from the desktops' specification and not yet tried: [tell us](https://github.com/kqesar/alltp-tracker/issues) how it goes.
- **sway, i3 and any other desktop with no such service**: bind a key in your window manager to `alltp-tracker --step advance:hookshot` (the tab lists a command for every control, with a Copy button). The same command works from a Stream Deck or a script, on every system. A second launch never opens a second tracker: it hands the step to the one running.

**Follow live on a phone.** The app can show your board on another device on the same network, updated as you track: a phone on the desk, a second screen, a co-commentator's laptop. Open **Share** (it stays available in Race Mode, with no link of the run in it), then turn on **Share the run live** under *Follow live on this network*, and scan the QR code with the phone's camera (or open the address under it). The phone shows the item grid, with the run timer for A Link to the Past, and a line that says whether it is live; nothing there can change your run. In Race Mode the app sends it, and the stream view's source, that grid and the timer alone, never the places you checked, their times or the seed; so it does while autotracking, set to connect, has not read the seed's race flag yet (the app's start, before SNI answers). Up to eight devices follow at once. Turn the switch off, or quit the app, and the devices watching say so (one that was asleep then keeps trying to reconnect); turning it on again makes a new link, so scan the new code. A device that loses the connection without a word notices within about 35 s and reconnects.

- **The same network**: the phone must be on the same Wi-Fi (or wired network) as the computer, not on mobile data. A guest Wi-Fi often keeps its devices apart.
- **The firewall**: on Windows, allow the app when asked, on private networks, and mark your Wi-Fi as private. On Linux with a firewall, open the port the dialog names (23075 unless taken): `sudo ufw allow 23075/tcp`, or `sudo firewall-cmd --add-port=23075/tcp` (until the next restart; run it again with `--permanent` to keep it).
- **The screen**: the phone's browser cannot keep its screen on for such a link; turn auto-lock off while watching (iPhone: Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock → Never; Android: Settings → Display → Screen timeout). If it sleeps anyway, it reconnects when it wakes.

**Show the run in OBS.** OBS can neither load the app's pages nor follow its storage, so the app serves the stream view to a Browser Source itself. Click **Stream view** in the toolbar, turn on **Serve the stream view to OBS**, and paste the address it shows (on this computer, `http://127.0.0.1:23076/?view=obs&token=…`) into a *Browser Source* in OBS. The dialog has the same options as the site's [stream view](#streaming-with-obs): *Show the run timer under the grid* for A Link to the Past, the sizes that keep every pixel whole, and `&bg=` for window capture.

- **Set up once.** The switch, the port and the address are remembered: the stream starts with the app, so the address in OBS stays the same after a restart, a reboot or an update. A source that is already open waits for the app and comes back by itself within a few seconds (it keeps its last grid meanwhile). **Start Rando Atlas before OBS**, though, or tick *Refresh browser when scene becomes active* in the source's properties: OBS does not retry a source whose page failed to load because the app was not running yet. While you track in the app, nothing else is needed: no dock, no browser.
- **The address is a secret.** It carries a token that keeps other devices on your network out, and OBS shows a source's address in its properties window: keep it off screen. **New addresses** (after a confirmation) replaces it and cuts off every source using the old one; those then say "This address is out of date", and need the new address pasted.
- **The port.** 23076, or the next free one up to 23085, unless another program holds them all; the dialog then says the stream moved to another port, a notice at the top says it too, once, if it happened when the app opened, and the address must be pasted again.
- **Only this computer, unless you allow more.** The stream listens on this computer alone, so OBS on it works with no firewall setting and nothing on your network can reach it. For OBS on another computer, tick **Allow other devices on the network**: the dialog then shows the address to use there (same secret, this computer's network address), and your firewall must let Rando Atlas through (the port is shown).

**Build your own overlay.** The same switch also turns on an **overlay API**: a WebSocket that tells a stream tool (a custom overlay in a Browser Source, Streamer.bot, a script) which items you hold, which bosses you have beaten, your timer and your go mode, and when each changes. It never says what a race keeps secret (checks, chests, prizes, medallion requirements, entrances, settings, keys), and in Race Mode it tells no time: no timer, and no time on a find or a boss (the race rules let a tracker log those times, but not hand them to other software). So it does too while autotracking, set to connect, has not read the seed's race flag yet. Its address, with its own secret, is in the same dialog under the Browser Source URL, with Copy; **New addresses** replaces it too. Frames, ids and a minimal overlay to start from: [overlay-api.md](overlay-api.md).

How the app is built and updated, how global shortcuts work, how live follow works and how the stream view is served: [desktop.md](desktop.md).
